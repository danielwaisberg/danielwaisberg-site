## Google Analytics — 2013 to 2018
 
Written as an Analytics Advocate, mostly on data analysis and visualization: how to build dashboards people can act on, and how to communicate the stories the numbers are actually telling.
 
### The book
 
**Google Analytics Integrations** (Wiley, 2015)

![Google Analytics Integrations book cover](/images/google-analytics-integrations-cover.png)


A hands-on guide to turning Google Analytics from a reporting tool into a central marketing analysis platform — integrating data from other Google products and from custom sources, so that a single view of a user, campaign, or product becomes possible.
 
The word *integration* comes from the Latin *integrare*, to make whole. That's the argument of the book: data tends to sit in parallel silos, like the warp threads on a loom, and it only becomes useful when something is woven across them.
 
Paul Muret, then VP of Engineering at Google, wrote in the foreword that the book offers <cite>critical insight into how to increase both the fidelity and the actionability of your Analytics data</cite>.
 
Published in 2015. Google Analytics has changed substantially since — treat it as a record of how the problem was framed at the time rather than as current documentation.
 
[Find it on Amazon →](https://www.amazon.com/Google-Analytics-Integrations-Daniel-Waisberg-dp-1119053064/dp/1119053064/)

**[Visualizing Brazil's presidential debate with Data Studio](https://blog.google/products/marketingplatform/analytics/visualizing-brazils-presidential-debate-datastudio/)** · September 2018 · Google Analytics blog

The Data Studio team built a public dashboard covering candidates, topics, and regional trends, pulling Google Trends data in real time. More than 150 journalists worked from it in the digital room; the event became the largest livestream ever on YouTube in Brazil.
